Why faucets drip and leak

Faucets have moving parts that wear. Rubber washers harden, cartridges scratch and O-rings crack. A drip from the spout means the internal seal is no longer closing, while a leak at the base or handle points to a seal or fitting problem.

Hard water speeds this up by leaving mineral deposits on the moving surfaces. If your faucet is stiff or sputters, deposits are often the cause.

Repair or replace

A faucet that is in good shape and drips is usually worth repairing. A faucet with a pitted finish, a cracked body or discontinued parts is better replaced. A replacement can also be a chance to upgrade to a fixture that is easier to clean and use.

If your sink has other problems, such as a slow drain or an aging disposal, we look at those at the same visit. See garbage disposal repair if that is part of the issue.

What a faucet repair visit includes

If the shutoff valves under the sink are corroded and will not close, we replace them as part of the job.

  1. Diagnose. We find where the water is escaping and identify the faucet type.
  2. Shut off. We close the supply stops under the sink.
  3. Repair or replace. We install the parts or the new faucet.
  4. Test. We check for leaks at the supply lines, spout and handles.

Water quality and fixtures in Montclair homes

Mineral deposits shorten the life of aerators and cartridges. Montclair publishes a water quality report that lists hardness, so you can see what your fixtures face. If the deposits are heavy, cleaning the aerator and using vinegar soaks help, and a softener helps more.

In older houses, supply lines and shutoff valves can be as old as the faucet. We check them while we are there, since replacing a small valve is cheaper than a flooded cabinet.

Codes and standards behind the work

Replacement cartridges and faucets should meet ASME A112.18.1, the standard for plumbing supply fittings, and lead free requirements apply to parts that touch drinking water.
