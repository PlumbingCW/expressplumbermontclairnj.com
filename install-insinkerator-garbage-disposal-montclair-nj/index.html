Install an InSinkErator Garbage Disposal in Montclair, NJ

Call (973) 488-5410

By the Montclair Express Plumbing team. Published November 12, 2026. Updated November 12, 2026.

Installing an InSinkErator disposal is a manageable project if the sink already has a disposal mount and an outlet nearby. Expect it to take a couple of hours, with the biggest steps being the mounting assembly, the drain connection and the electrical connection. A first install in a cramped Montclair cabinet may take longer.

This guide walks through the stages and the mistakes we see most. If any step feels beyond you, call (973) 488-5410 and we will install it.

Before you start

  • Turn off power at the breaker and confirm with a tester.
  • Check whether the outlet is switched or hard wired.
  • Clear the cabinet under the sink.
  • Read the installation instructions for your model, since mounts differ.
  • Have a bucket, plumber putty, a screwdriver and a hex key on hand.

The main steps

  1. Remove the old flange or disposal. Disconnect the drain, loosen the mount and lift out the old unit.
  2. Mount the new flange. Set plumber putty under the sink flange and tighten the mounting assembly from below.
  3. Attach the disposal. Lift the unit onto the mount and twist it into place.
  4. Connect the drain. Align the discharge outlet and attach the drain trap.
  5. Wire it. Connect the power cord or hard wire according to the instructions.
  6. Test. Run water, check every joint and run the disposal.

Older kitchens in Montclair

Many Montclair kitchens have older drain lines, sometimes galvanized or cast iron, and these can be rough inside. A disposal adds food waste to a line that may already be narrowing. Consider a drain check at the same time as the install, so the new unit does not blame its drain.

When to hire a plumber

Call a plumber if the cabinet is tight, the drain needs reworking or you need a new outlet. We install disposals and look at the drain at the same visit. See garbage disposal repair for related work and drain cleaning if the sink backs up.

Choosing the right model

Models differ in motor power, noise and how they are fed. Continuous feed units run while the switch is on, and batch feed units run only when a stopper is in place. Batch feed is safer around children, and continuous feed is quicker for daily use.

Larger motors handle tougher scraps and run more smoothly, and many have extra insulation for lower noise. Check that the mounting system fits your sink and that the unit fits in the cabinet space under it.

Electrical and dishwasher connections

Most disposals plug into an outlet under the sink, often switched from the wall. If the sink has no outlet, someone has to add one or hard wire the unit, and that is electrical work. Never run an extension cord to a disposal.

If a dishwasher drains through the disposal, knock out the plug in the dishwasher inlet before you attach the hose. Skipping this step is the most common cause of a dishwasher that will not drain after an install.

Step by step: removing the old unit

Turn off power at the breaker, then unplug the unit or disconnect the wires. Place a bucket under the trap, loosen the drain pipe and the dishwasher hose. Support the disposal with one hand while you turn the mounting ring to release it, because it is heavier than it looks.

Clean the sink flange of old putty before the new one goes in. Dried putty left behind is the most common reason a new disposal leaks from the top.

Testing and first use

Fill the sink with a little water and watch every joint for drips. Run the disposal with cold water for half a minute and check again under the sink. Feel the underside of the flange and the drain connection for dampness with a dry paper towel.

If a joint weeps, tighten it slightly and retest. If the dishwasher connection is new, run a short dishwasher cycle and confirm it drains.

Care tips once it is running

Run cold water before, during and after use so scraps clear the trap. Skip grease, fibrous peels and large bones. A few ice cubes now and then help knock residue off the grinding parts.

If the unit hums but does not spin, switch it off and use the hex key socket on the underside to free it. Press the reset button before turning power back on.

Codes and standards behind the work

A disposal on a household circuit falls under the National Electrical Code (NFPA 70), and the dishwasher drain must be routed with an air gap or high loop as local code requires.
