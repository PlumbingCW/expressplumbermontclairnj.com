Why correct installation matters

Plumbing hides inside walls and under floors, so mistakes show up years later. Poor slope on a drain, an unvented fixture or a poorly soldered joint can cause leaks and slow drains. These faults are cheaper to prevent than to find.

New Jersey plumbing work is done by a licensed plumber and many installations involve a permit and an inspection. We handle that process so you are not guessing about what applies.

Planning around an older house

Montclair has many older homes, and the first surprise in a remodel is usually what is behind the wall. You may find galvanized pipe, cast iron stacks or additions piped by a previous owner. We look at the existing plumbing before we price new work.

If the existing lines are failing, we say so before you build around them. That conversation can lead to repiping before the new bathroom goes in.

How an installation project runs

  1. Walk the site. We look at the existing plumbing and plan routes.
  2. Quote the job. You get a written price that lists what is included.
  3. Rough in. Lines and valves go in before walls are closed.
  4. Final connections. Fixtures are set and tested, and inspection is scheduled when needed.

Planning a new fixture or line

Good installs start with a look at the space, the water and drain locations and the code. A new bathroom, a kitchen island sink or a laundry hookup each needs the right sized lines and vents. Moving a fixture far from its drain adds cost, so layout choices matter.

Tell us what you want and what you have already bought. We will say if the fixture suits the space and what else the job needs.

Permits, inspections and warranties

Many installations need a permit, and the work may be inspected before walls close. A permit protects you when you sell or file an insurance claim. Ask who pulls it and what the inspector will look for.

Keep manuals, receipts and warranty cards together. A fixture warranty can depend on proof that it was installed correctly.

Codes and standards behind the work

New fixtures and lines must meet the International Plumbing Code as adopted under the New Jersey Uniform Construction Code, and many installations need a permit and an inspection before walls are closed.
