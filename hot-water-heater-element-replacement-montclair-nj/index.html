Hot Water Heater Element Replacement in Montclair, NJ

Call (973) 488-5410

By the Montclair Express Plumbing team. Published November 12, 2026. Updated November 12, 2026.

An electric water heater that runs out of hot water quickly, or gives none at all, often has a failed heating element. Most electric tanks have two, an upper and a lower, and each can fail on its own. A plumber can test both with a meter in a few minutes and replace the bad one in a single visit.

This guide explains the symptoms, what the repair involves and how to decide between a new element and a new heater. If you are in Montclair and the tank is leaking, skip ahead and call (973) 488-5410 because a leaking tank cannot be fixed with a new element.

Signs of a failed element

The symptoms depend on which element failed. A bad upper element leaves you with no hot water at all, because the upper element heats the top of the tank first. A bad lower element usually gives you a short supply of hot water that runs out fast.

  • Lukewarm water that never gets fully hot.
  • Hot water that runs out within a few minutes.
  • A breaker that trips when the heater runs.
  • A heater that worked until a power flicker.

Element or thermostat?

Thermostats and elements fail in similar ways, so testing matters. Replacing the wrong part wastes money and leaves you without hot water. We test continuity on each element and check that the thermostats are switching power on and off.

SymptomMore likelyHow it is confirmed
No hot water at allUpper thermostat or element, or a tripped high limitMeter test and reset check

Water too hotStuck thermostatThermostat test
Breaker keeps trippingElement grounded to the tankInsulation resistance test

What replacement involves

The power is shut off at the breaker and tested at the heater. The tank is partly drained so that water does not spill when the element is removed. The old element comes out with a socket, a new gasket and element go in, and the tank is refilled and checked for leaks before power returns.

Skipping the refill step burns out a new element in seconds, because elements must be covered by water before they get power. It is one of the main mistakes we see on do it yourself jobs.

Montclair water and element life

Hard water leaves scale on elements and shortens their life. Montclair publishes an annual water quality report that lists hardness, so you can read the number for your supply instead of guessing. If it shows hard water, flushing the tank each year and checking the anode rod can extend the life of a new element.

When a new element is not the answer

A leaking tank cannot be repaired. A heater with heavy sediment and rust colored water is near the end of its life. In those cases, water heater replacement makes more sense than another element.

If your heater is old and has already needed an element or thermostat, ask for both prices. We give you the repair price and the replacement price and let you decide.

Tools and safety before you start

You need a multimeter, an element wrench or a deep socket, a new element and gasket of the right wattage and voltage, and a garden hose for draining. The data plate on the heater lists the wattage and voltage, so match them exactly. A wrong element can trip the breaker or burn out fast.

Power comes first. Switch the breaker off, remove the access panel and test the wires with a meter to confirm that no power is present. Many injuries on this job come from assuming the breaker label is correct.

An element repair makes sense when the tank is sound. The table shows how we weigh the options when we meet a customer with a cold heater.

Codes and standards behind the work

Electric water heater circuits fall under the National Electrical Code (NFPA 70), and element wattage and voltage must match the data plate that the manufacturer prints on the tank.
