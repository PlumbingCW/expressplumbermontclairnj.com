Stopping an overflowing toilet

If the bowl is rising, lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop more water from entering. Then turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. These two steps take a few seconds and prevent most floor damage.

Once the water is stopped, call (973) 488-5410. If other fixtures are also slow or gurgling, mention it, because that suggests a deeper clog than the toilet itself.

Common toilet problems and what causes them

Most toilet repairs are quick once the cause is known. The trick is finding the cause rather than replacing parts one at a time.

When a clog is bigger than the toilet

A toilet clogged with a lot of paper is usually a local problem. A toilet that backs up while the shower drains slowly is a sign of a branch or main line problem. The plumber looks at the pattern, not only the bowl.

If the toilet clears and the problem returns, we may recommend drain cleaning or a camera look. We do not keep clearing the same toilet without finding the cause.

Insurance generally covers water damage from a sudden overflow more readily than the cost of the repair itself. Ask your carrier. Call (973) 488-5410 and we will give you the price before we start.

Repair or replace the toilet

A toilet with a cracked bowl or tank should be replaced. A toilet that is old but sound often responds well to new internal parts. Water efficient models save water and many flush better than older ones.

We help you decide without pressure. If you want a new toilet, we install it and haul away the old one.

Older bathrooms in Montclair homes

Montclair houses with original bathrooms often have older toilets, brass supply connections and cast iron or galvanized drain stacks. These materials can complicate a simple repair. Corroded shutoff valves may fail when turned, so we check them first and replace them when needed.

For second floor bathrooms in older houses, a slow flush can trace to venting or the drain stack, not the toilet. We look at the whole system, which saves repeated calls.

Codes and standards behind the work

Toilet fixtures are made to ASME A112.19.2 and fill valves to ASSE 1002, so replacement parts should match those standards.
