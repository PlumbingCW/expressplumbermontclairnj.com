Signs of a failed water service line

The service line is buried, so you often see the effect, not the pipe. Sudden loss of pressure, no water at all, a soggy patch in the yard or a basement wall that is damp near where the line enters are all clues. A meter that moves with every fixture off is another strong one.

If you can hear water running near the meter or see water pooling at the foundation, call (973) 488-5410 right away. A leak that large can wash out soil and undermine slabs or walkways.

Who owns which part of the line

The municipal main runs under the street, and your service line runs from there to the house. The line on your property is generally the owner responsibility, and the utility handles the part under the street. The meter location is a useful marker, and we help you work out where the break falls.

If the break is on the utility side, we help you document it and contact the right office. Spending time on this early saves a dig on the wrong side.

Lead service lines and what Montclair homeowners should know

New Jersey requires water systems to replace lead service lines, and homeowners in older areas may be affected. If your line is replaced as part of a program, it is worth checking whether the utility covers its side and what you are responsible for. When we repair a line, we look at the material and tell you what it is.

This matters for repairs because replacing a short section of a lead line with copper can complicate matters. If the line is lead, we talk through the options before we dig.

What affects the price of water line repair

Water line work is priced to the length and the surface above the pipe.

  • Length and depth of the line.
  • What is above it: lawn, driveway, walkway or patio.
  • Pipe material and size.
  • Permits and utility locating.
  • After hours dispatch for emergencies.

Some homeowners insurance riders cover service line failures, while standard policies often exclude it. Ask your carrier. Call (973) 488-5410 and we will give you a price after we locate the break.

How the repair proceeds

  1. Locate the line and the leak. We find the path of the pipe and the point of failure.
  2. Call for utility marking. Underground utilities are marked before digging.
  3. Open and repair. We dig the smallest trench that allows a lasting repair.
  4. Test and restore. We pressure test, backfill and restore the surface as agreed.

Codes and standards behind the work

Water service lines are installed to the International Plumbing Code, and a repair or replacement can need a permit under the New Jersey Uniform Construction Code and coordination with the water supplier.
