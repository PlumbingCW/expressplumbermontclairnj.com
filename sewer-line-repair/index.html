What a sewer line emergency looks like

A true sewer line failure shows up in the lowest drains first. Basement floor drains gurgle, toilets bubble when you run the washer, or wastewater rises where it should not. Several fixtures failing at the same time is the clearest sign that the problem is in the main line.

Stop all water use and keep people away from affected areas. Sewage carries bacteria, so avoid contact and call (973) 488-5410 so a plumber can confirm the cause.

Blocked or broken? The camera answers that

A blockage can be cleared. A broken or collapsed pipe has to be repaired, and the two look similar from the house. We run a camera through the line to tell them apart and to mark the location of the problem.

You see the footage and we explain it. Our sewer inspection page describes that process in more detail.

Why sewer lines fail

Older lines under Montclair yards are often clay, cast iron or fiber pipe that was set many decades ago. Clay joints open as the ground shifts, and roots follow the moisture into the gaps. Cast iron rusts from the inside and can flake until the line narrows or cracks.

A belly is a low spot where the pipe has settled and holds water, so waste collects instead of flowing away. Bellies cause repeating clogs, and they do not respond well to cleaning. Knowing which cause you have decides the repair.

Sewer repair options compared

Not every option fits every line. The camera footage and the pipe material set what is realistic, and we tell you why we recommend one over another.

Spot repairDig down and replace a short damaged sectionOne broken joint or short crack

Trenchless work is not available for every pipe. Heavy offsets and collapsed sections usually need digging, and we explain that before you decide.

What affects the price of sewer line repair

Sewer work is one of the larger repairs a home can need, so we put the number in writing before work starts.

  • Length of the damaged section: a short repair costs less than a long run.
  • Depth and location: a line under a driveway or a mature tree takes more effort.
  • Method: lining, bursting and open digging carry different costs and different restoration needs.
  • Access to the line: a clean outside cleanout makes everything easier.
  • Permits and inspections: sewer work usually involves the municipality.

Insurance often treats a gradual sewer failure as maintenance, but sudden damage inside the house from a backup can fall under optional sewer backup coverage. Check your policy before a loss and ask about it. Call (973) 488-5410 for a number on your line.

Sewer rules and permits in Montclair

Work on the line that runs to the street usually involves the township, and the rules for what connects where are set locally. Local sewer rules generally keep stormwater out of the sanitary sewer, so we ask where a sump or roof drain connects before we recommend any changes. In New Jersey plumbing work is done by a licensed plumber, and we handle the permit questions that come with a repair.

The practical point is simple. Before you dig, you want one company that knows the process, will tell you who needs to inspect, and will not start before the paperwork is in order.

After the heavy storms that hit northern New Jersey

The remnants of Hurricane Ida in September 2021 flooded many basements across northern New Jersey, and sewer backups were part of the story for some homes. A backup that only happens during heavy rain points to stormwater getting into the system or to a line that is too slow to drain. A camera can show whether the house line is the weak point.

If your sewer backs up in storms, ask us about a backwater valve and a sump pump check. Our sump pump page explains how those fit together.

Codes and standards behind the work

Sewer pipe and fittings must meet the International Plumbing Code and the ASTM standards it references, and a repair or replacement of a buried line needs a permit and inspection under the New Jersey Uniform Construction Code.
