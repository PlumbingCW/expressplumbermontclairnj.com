Water heater emergencies that cannot wait

Two situations deserve a call right away. The first is water on the floor around the tank, and the second is a gas smell near the unit. For a leak, turn off the cold supply valve above the heater and, for an electric unit, switch off its breaker. For gas, leave the house and call the gas utility before you call anyone else.

A heater with no hot water and no leak can usually wait until the first available appointment. Either way, call (973) 488-5410 and describe what you see so dispatch can set the right priority.

Common reasons a water heater stops heating

Most heater faults come from a short list of parts. Knowing the list helps you judge whether a repair makes sense.

SymptomLikely causeTypical outcome
No hot water, electric tankFailed heating element or thermostatParts repair
No hot water, gas tankPilot or thermocouple, igniter or gas control valveRepair if the tank is sound
Water too hot or too coolThermostat setting or a failed thermostatAdjustment or part swap
Rumbling or poppingSediment baked onto the bottom of the tankFlush now, replace later if severe
Water at the baseFitting leak or a failed tankFitting repair or replacement

A leak from a fitting on top of the tank is a repair. A leak from the tank itself is not, because the steel has failed and no part can fix it.

Repair or replace: a practical way to decide

Age is the first question, and the data plate on the tank shows the manufacture date in the serial number. Tanks that are well past a decade old often cost more to keep alive than to replace. The second question is what failed, because a thermostat is cheap while a rusted tank is final.

We give you both numbers when the choice is close. If a repair buys another few years at a fair price, we recommend it. If the repair would only postpone a larger bill, we say so.

What a repair visit looks like

Most repairs finish in a single visit when the part is on the truck. Specialty parts for older units may take longer, and we tell you that up front.

  1. We test first. The plumber checks power or gas, the thermostat settings, the relief valve and the supply connections.
  2. We find the failed part. Elements, thermostats, igniters and valves each have a test that confirms the fault.
  3. You hear the price. We explain the repair, the parts involved and the alternative before we start.
  4. We repair and verify. We bring the heater to temperature, check for leaks and confirm safe venting.

What affects the cost of water heater repair

Repair prices change with the part, the fuel and the time you call.

  • The failed part: an element or thermostat is a modest repair, a gas control valve is more.
  • Fuel type: gas units involve venting and gas safety checks that add steps.
  • Age and parts availability: older heaters sometimes need parts that are harder to find.
  • Access: tight basement closets slow the work.
  • Time of day: after hours service involves a crew dispatched outside normal schedules.

Insurance usually covers a heater that fails suddenly and floods a room, while the heater itself is treated as maintenance. Check with your carrier. For a number on your specific unit, call (973) 488-5410.

Older homes and water heaters in Montclair

Many Montclair homes have a heater in a basement or utility closet that was fitted around the original boiler. These spaces are often tight, which matters for access, venting and clearances. A heater that fits poorly tends to be harder to service and more prone to rust from basement dampness.

After the flooding that Hurricane Ida brought to northern New Jersey in September 2021, many homeowners learned how quickly a basement heater can be damaged by standing water. If your heater sits low and your basement has taken on water before, raising it on a stand or choosing a different location is worth discussing during a repair visit.

Tankless water heaters need different repairs

Tankless units fail in different ways, often from scale inside the heat exchanger or from error codes tied to flow and venting. We repair them, and we also explain when the issue is the supply rather than the unit.

Our tankless water heater page covers installation and sizing. If a tankless unit is struggling, it helps to know the model and whatever code the display shows when you call.

Codes and standards behind the work

Gas storage water heaters are listed to ANSI Z21.10.1 and installed under the National Fuel Gas Code (NFPA 54), and a temperature and pressure relief valve is required on every tank.
