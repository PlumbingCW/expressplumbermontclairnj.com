What a sewer camera can show

A small camera on a flexible cable travels through the line and sends video to a monitor. The plumber watches the screen and notes what shows at each distance. The footage shows cracks, offset joints, roots, grease, scale, bellies and collapsed sections.

You get more than a yes or no. You see where the problem is, how serious it looks and what each repair option would address.

When an inspection is worth doing

Buyers often ask for an inspection before closing because sewer repairs are some of the largest costs a house can hide. A camera look can bring those facts into negotiations before the sale is final.

  • The same drain clogs again and again.
  • You are buying an older home and want to know the condition of the line.
  • You have a sewer smell with no obvious source.
  • There are mature trees near the line.
  • A previous repair needs to be confirmed or documented.

How the inspection works

If the line is full of debris, we may need to clean it before the camera can pass. We explain that before adding steps to the job.

  1. Find access. We use a cleanout, or pull a toilet if no cleanout exists.
  2. Run the camera. The camera travels toward the street while the monitor shows the line.
  3. Note the findings. We mark locations of defects and record footage.
  4. Review together. You see the footage and we explain the cause and the options.

What affects the price of an inspection

A camera inspection is a diagnostic service with a straightforward scope.

  • Length of the line from the cleanout to the street.
  • Whether the line needs cleaning before the camera can pass.
  • Access: a cleanout speeds the job.
  • A written report or recorded footage for a sale.
  • After hours dispatch for urgent problems.

Insurance usually does not pay for an inspection ordered only as a precaution, but it may be required in a claim. Ask your carrier if you are documenting a loss. Call (973) 488-5410 and we will tell you the price before we come.

Montclair homes and why a camera look helps

Many Montclair houses have sewer lines that were laid a century ago, and the pipe material and joint style vary from block to block. A camera shows what you actually have rather than what the age of the house suggests. For a buyer looking at a home near Mountainside Park or in the South End, that visibility can change the negotiation.

The same logic helps an owner. If the footage shows a healthy line, you avoid an unnecessary dig. If it shows a failing section, you can plan the repair on your schedule and not during an emergency.

Codes and standards behind the work

Camera findings are described using terms from the NASSCO Pipeline Assessment framework, a widely used way of grading cracks, offsets, roots and deposits in buried pipe.
