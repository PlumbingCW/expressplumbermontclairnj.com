What to do when a drain backs up tonight

When water rises in a shower while the washing machine drains, the blockage sits past the point where those lines meet. That usually means a branch or main line problem, and a plunger will not reach it. The first few minutes matter because every gallon you add ends up somewhere in the house.

Stop using water in every fixture, then check the lowest drain in the home. If a basement floor drain or a first-floor tub is gurgling or filling, tell dispatch so the plumber arrives ready for a main line.

  1. Stop the water. No flushing, no laundry, no dishwasher until the line is open.
  2. Check the lowest fixtures. Floor drains and basement tubs show a main line backup first.
  3. Move belongings. Lift rugs, boxes and cords off the floor near the drain.
  4. Call us. Describe which fixtures are affected so we bring the right machine.

Why drains clog in Montclair houses

Montclair has many homes from the early 1900s, and older drain lines are rougher inside than modern pipe. Grease, hair, soap film and scale catch on that rough surface and narrow the opening a little more each year. A slow drain that you have lived with for months is often a line that is already half closed.

Mature trees are part of what makes streets in Upper Montclair and around Watchung Plaza so pleasant, and their roots go looking for moisture. Roots enter at joints in older clay and cast iron sewer lines, which is why a house with a clean kitchen drain can still back up in the basement. When the symptoms point to the yard line, we will tell you that before any cutting starts.

What happens on a drain cleaning visit

The plumber starts by asking which fixtures are slow and when the problem began. That conversation narrows the location faster than any tool, and it saves you time on the bill.

Next comes access. We use a cleanout when there is one, or pull a toilet or open a trap if there is not, and we protect floors while we work. Once the line is open we run water to confirm it moves, and we explain what caused the clog so you can decide about follow-up work.

If the same line clogs again within a few weeks, we recommend a camera look. A repeating clog is usually a sign of a belly, a broken joint or roots rather than bad luck.

What affects the price of drain cleaning

Drain cleaning has no single price because the work ranges from a ten minute branch clog to a main line job with a long run of cable. We quote after we know what we are dealing with, and you approve the number before work begins.

  • Which line is blocked: a fixture branch costs less than a main sewer line.
  • Access: a cleanout speeds the job, while pulling a toilet adds time.
  • Distance and depth: a long run to the street takes more cable and more time.
  • The cause: grease and hair clear quickly, roots and scale take heavier equipment.
  • Time of day: nights and holidays involve dispatching a crew outside normal hours.

Signs the clog is in the main line

A single slow sink points to that fixture. When several fixtures act up together, the blockage is further downstream.

Gurgling in other drains

A toilet flush that bubbles in the tub means air is being pushed through a partly blocked main.

Water at the lowest drain

The basement floor drain fills first because it is the lowest opening on the system.

Several fixtures slow at once

Showers, sinks and the washer all draining badly points past the branch lines.

Odor from the floor drain

A sewer smell with a slow drain can mean trapped debris or a dry trap that needs attention.

Keeping drains open between visits

Small habits make the biggest difference in a house with older pipe. None of them require tools.

  • Wipe greasy pans into the trash before washing them.
  • Use a hair catcher in every shower and tub.
  • Run hot water for a minute after washing dishes.
  • Keep wipes, even ones labeled flushable, out of the toilet.
  • Avoid chemical drain openers, which can sit in a pipe and damage fittings and fixtures.

Codes and standards behind the work

Cleanouts, traps and vents are required by the International Plumbing Code, and we use them as the access points when we clear a line.
