How tankless heaters work

When you open a hot tap, a flow sensor starts a burner or electric elements and heats water as it passes through a heat exchanger. The water never sits in a tank, so you do not run out the way you do with a tank that empties. The limit is flow rate, which is how much hot water the unit can produce at once.

That limit matters in a busy house. Two showers and a dishwasher at the same time can exceed what a single unit delivers, so sizing is the most important step in any tankless project.

Tankless versus tank: an honest comparison

Tankless is not always the better choice. It makes the most sense where space is limited, where gas service can support it, or where you want to avoid the risk of a tank failure in a finished basement.

What we check before recommending a unit

A good tankless installation starts with the house, not the product. We look at the gas line size, the route for venting, the electrical panel if the unit is electric, and the water quality. These checks decide whether the project is simple or whether upgrades are required.

  • Gas pipe size and meter capacity for gas units.
  • Venting route and clearances.
  • Panel capacity and a dedicated circuit for electric units.
  • Hot water demand during your busiest hours.
  • Water hardness, which affects scaling and maintenance.

Water quality and maintenance in Montclair

Scale builds on heat exchangers, and tankless units are less forgiving of it than tanks. Montclair publishes an annual water quality report that lists hardness, so you can read the figure for your supply. If it shows hard water, annual descaling and sometimes a softener protect the heat exchanger.

We set up a simple maintenance plan when we install a unit, including descaling and filter cleaning. Most error codes we see on neglected units trace back to scale or blocked inlet screens, so routine care avoids many service calls.

Repairing a tankless unit

A tankless unit that shows an error code, runs lukewarm or cycles on and off is often fixable. Common causes include scale buildup, blocked screens, venting problems and failed sensors.

We repair units we install and units installed by others. If the repair would cost close to a replacement on an older unit, we say so. For emergencies with no hot water, our water heater repair team can help while you decide.

What affects the price of a tankless project

Tankless installation tends to cost more than a tank swap because the supporting work is larger.

  • Unit capacity and brand.
  • Gas line upsizing or electrical service upgrades.
  • New venting or relocation of the unit.
  • Water treatment or isolation valves for descaling.
  • Permits and inspections.

Insurance generally does not cover a planned upgrade. Some utilities and manufacturers offer rebates on efficient models, so ask before you buy. Call (973) 488-5410 for a price built around your house.

Codes and standards behind the work

Gas tankless units are listed to ANSI Z21.10.3 and vented and piped under the National Fuel Gas Code (NFPA 54), and the manufacturer instructions set the clearances and the vent type.

Which Montclair homes suit tankless

Smaller homes and condos near Bloomfield Avenue and Lackawanna Plaza often benefit most, because wall space is better used than a floor tank. Larger homes in Upper Montclair can also be a good fit when the gas service is sized for it and the venting route is clear. Homes with older chimneys need careful planning because the old flue may not suit a modern unit.
