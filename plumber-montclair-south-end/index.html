The South End at a glance

The township lists the South End among its six business districts, and the Martin Luther King Jr. Peace Garden is located there. Local park guides place Nishuane Park, Glenfield Park and Canterbury Park in this part of town. Orange Road, Grove Street and Bloomfield Avenue are the main roads people use to describe it.

Sources do not give exact boundaries for the South End, so we describe it by those landmarks and ask for your street address when you call.

Houses, townhomes and condos need different questions

In a single-family house, the plumbing from the street to the fixtures is yours to maintain. In a townhome or condo, some pipes belong to the association and some to the unit owner, and the answer depends on your documents.

Before you call a plumber for a leak in an attached home, check whether the water is coming from your unit or from a neighbor. If it is the neighbor, tell your property manager first.

Before a plumber arrives

Association rules can affect when a plumber may work and whether the building water has to be shut off. Ask your manager if you live in a shared building.

  • Locate the shutoff for the fixture, and the main shutoff for the unit or house.
  • Clear the area under sinks so the plumber can reach the pipes.
  • Check whether a condo or townhome association has rules about work hours or notice.
  • Move valuables away from wet floors and walls.

Local rules to know

Montclair Township runs its own Water Bureau and Sewer Utility, so questions about the water meter or the line to the street go to the township. Permits for many repairs and replacements go through the township construction office.

We can tell you which jobs usually involve a permit when you call, so you know what to expect before work starts.

Codes and standards behind the work

Plumbing repairs and replacements in Montclair follow the International Plumbing Code as adopted under the New Jersey Uniform Construction Code, and permits are handled by the township construction office.
