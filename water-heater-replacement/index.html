When a failing water heater is an emergency

A heater that leaks from the tank body cannot be repaired, because the steel liner has failed. Water on the basement floor, rust colored water at the tap or a tank that is hot at the pipes but cold inside all point toward replacement. Once water is escaping, the question is how quickly you can shut off the supply and get a plumber to the house.

Close the cold water valve on top of the heater and turn off power at the breaker or the gas valve at the unit. Then call (973) 488-5410, and dispatch will take the details.

Signs your water heater is near the end

The date on the data plate tells you more than the story of the house. Heaters that were installed by a previous owner are easy to misjudge, so check the plate before assuming the age.

  • The unit is well past ten years old by the date in its serial number.
  • Rust colored hot water, which suggests the tank is corroding.
  • Water pooling at the base that does not come from a fitting.
  • Frequent repairs for thermostats, elements or igniters.
  • Rumbling that returns after flushing, which points to heavy sediment.

How a replacement visit goes

A straightforward swap can often be done in a day when the new tank fits the same space and connections. Changing fuel type or relocating the unit takes longer, and we explain the schedule up front.

  1. Confirm the diagnosis. We test the unit and rule out cheaper repairs before recommending a new heater.
  2. Size the new unit. We match the tank size and recovery rate to the household.
  3. Drain and remove the old tank. We protect floors, drain the tank safely and haul the old unit away.
  4. Set, connect and test. We connect water, gas or power, venting, relief valve and drain, then verify temperature and leaks.

What affects the price of water heater replacement

Replacement quotes depend on more than the tank. We list what is included so that you can compare quotes fairly.

  • Type and size of the new heater.
  • Whether venting, gas lines or electrical circuits need updating to meet code.
  • Condition of shutoff valves, supply connections and the expansion tank.
  • Access to the basement or closet and the route for hauling the old tank out.
  • Time of day if the swap happens as an emergency after hours.

Homeowners insurance may cover water damage when a tank bursts but usually not the cost of the new heater. Ask your carrier about the damage and keep photos. Call (973) 488-5410 for a price on your replacement.

Water quality in Montclair and how long a tank lasts

Mineral content in water leaves scale on heating elements and the bottom of tanks, and tanks in harder water areas often work harder for the same hot water. Montclair publishes a water quality report each year that lists hardness, so you can read the number for your supply rather than guess. If the report shows hard water, a softener or regular flushing can make a new tank last longer.

We bring this up during a replacement because it affects the next heater too. The anode rod, which protects the steel tank from corrosion, is worth checking and replacing on schedule.

Codes and standards behind the work

A replacement heater is installed under the International Plumbing Code and the National Fuel Gas Code (NFPA 54), with a relief valve, discharge pipe and permit where the New Jersey Uniform Construction Code requires them.
