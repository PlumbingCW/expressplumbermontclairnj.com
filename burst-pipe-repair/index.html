What to do in the first ten minutes

Every minute counts when a pipe has burst. The steps below limit the damage while a plumber is on the way. You do not need to find the break, only stop the water supply.

Step 1

Find the main shutoff.

It is usually where the water line enters the house, near the meter in a basement or utility room. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Step 2

Open a low faucet.

Open a tap on the lowest floor to drain the pipes and relieve pressure.

Step 3

Cut power in wet areas.

If water is near outlets or the panel, switch off breakers only if you can reach them without standing in water.

Step 4

Call us and move belongings.

Call (973) 488-5410, then lift valuables and move rugs out of the way.

If water is coming through a ceiling light, stay away and call for help. Do not touch electrical fixtures that are wet.

Why pipes burst

Freezing is the most common cause in New Jersey winters. Water expands when it freezes and splits the pipe, though the leak often only appears when the ice thaws. Corrosion, pressure spikes and old joints cause bursts in other seasons.

Pipes along exterior walls, in unheated basements and in crawlspaces are the most at risk. A hose left connected outside can also freeze back into the house pipe.

Which pipe broke and how we repair it

We repair with permanent materials and not with temporary clamps unless you need a short-term stop. If several sections are weak, we say so before leaving.

After the water is stopped

Stopping the leak is the first step, and drying the house is the second. Wet drywall, insulation and flooring can grow mold within a couple of days. We can stop the water and make the repair, and a water damage restoration company can dry the structure.

Take photos of the damage and the broken pipe before anything is moved. Your insurance carrier will ask for them.

What affects the price of burst pipe repair

Prices vary with the pipe, the location and the amount of damage.

  • Type and size of the pipe.
  • Access: a break in a finished wall takes more work than one in an open basement.
  • Length of damaged pipe.
  • Whether other sections are at risk.
  • After hours dispatch for emergency calls.

Homeowners insurance often covers sudden water damage from a burst pipe, though it may exclude the pipe repair itself. Call your carrier early and keep the receipts. Call (973) 488-5410 for a price on your repair.

Winter in northern New Jersey and your pipes

Cold snaps in Essex County can drop quickly, and houses with pipes along exterior walls or unheated crawlspaces are at risk. In Montclair, older homes with additions often have pipes that run through cold spaces the original builders did not expect. If you are going away in winter, keep the heat on and open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls.

If a pipe has frozen but not burst, see leak detection after thawing to check for hidden damage. Thawing a frozen pipe slowly with warm air is safer than flame.

Codes and standards behind the work

Replacement pipe and fittings should meet the International Plumbing Code and the material standards it references, and larger repairs can need a permit under the New Jersey Uniform Construction Code.
