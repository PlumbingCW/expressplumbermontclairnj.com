When the pump fails during a storm

A sump pit that fills with no pump running means water will rise to the floor. Check whether the pump has power, then check the float, which may be stuck against the wall of the pit. If you can safely free it, the pump may start. If not, call (973) 488-5410.

Do not stand in water around electrical equipment. Keep belongings off the floor and, if the water reaches outlets, stay out of the basement until power is shut off.

Why sump pumps fail

Many pumps are simply old. A pump that has run for a decade is on borrowed time.

Montclair, storms and basements

In early September 2021 the remnants of Hurricane Ida flooded basements and streets across northern New Jersey, and Montclair homeowners were among those affected. Storms like that test every sump pump in the area. A pump that has not been tested recently may fail when it is needed.

The practical lesson is to test the pump before the storm and to have a backup if the power goes out. A battery backup keeps the pit pumping when the grid fails, which is exactly when the water is highest.

Where the water goes

Local sewer rules generally keep stormwater out of the sanitary sewer. That means a sump discharge should go to the yard or a storm connection where allowed, not into the house drain. We ask where yours goes before we recommend changes, and we move it if it ties into the wrong place.

Discharge lines can freeze in winter, and a frozen line stops the pump from working. We route and slope lines to reduce that risk.

What affects the price of sump pump work

A repair and a new installation are different jobs.

  • Repair or replacement of the pump.
  • Pump size and type, and whether a battery backup is added.
  • Pit condition and discharge routing.
  • Electrical outlet and circuit.
  • After hours dispatch during a storm.

Standard homeowners policies often exclude flooding and may not cover sump pump overflow unless you carry a rider. Ask your carrier before storm season. Call (973) 488-5410 for a price on your pump.

Installing a new sump system

A new system can be planned alongside basement waterproofing when water enters in several places.

  1. Assess the basement. We check where water enters and where the pit should sit.
  2. Set the pit. We cut the floor, set the basin and pump and connect the discharge.
  3. Add a check valve and backup. A check valve keeps water from flowing back, and a battery backup protects against power failure.
  4. Test. We run water into the pit to confirm that the pump starts and discharges.

Codes and standards behind the work

Pump outlets should be on a GFCI protected circuit under the National Electrical Code (NFPA 70).
